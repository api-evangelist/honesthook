# HonestHook

> Profiles and posts from the major social networks, through one API. One key, one response shape; rate limits, retries and platform changes are on us. Clean JSON for your app or your AI agent. Free key, 1,000 credits a month, no card.

HonestHook keeps an hourly archive of what is rising in a niche: rank, points
and comment counts, per item, never deleted. Every other API in this market
answers what is trending right now and discards it -- their caches run 2 to 30
minutes. This one answers what GAINED TRACTION, which cannot be reconstructed
later because the past was not photographed.

Best for: measuring whether a topic, tool or launch is accelerating or dying,
when a single snapshot cannot tell you which.

Status: live, self-serve. Free key: 1,000 free CREDITS per month, then paid credits.
Credits are the unit, not calls: `archive/trends` costs 1 (so 1,000 of those),
`archive/movers` costs 10 (so 100 of those). The allowance renews on the 1st;
purchased credits never expire. The archive
began on 2026-09-05 -- ask /api/v1/nichos how deep it is for your niche before
you build on it. We would rather say that than have you find out.

## What makes it different

Most social data tools answer *what is trending right now*. HonestHook keeps the
history, so it can answer *what is rising and what is already dying* — a question
that only a tool which started recording earlier can answer. Competitors cache
between 2 and 30 minutes and throw the past away; the archive is the moat.

## API

Base URL: https://honesthook.com/api/v1. Auth is `Authorization: Bearer hk_live_...`.
Get a key with `POST /api/v1/chaves` and `{"email":"you@company.com"}` — free,
no card, returned once and stored only as a SHA-256.

Billing is per call, in credits, debited inside the same database transaction
that reads the archive:

| endpoint | credits | charges on an empty result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `archive/coverage` | 0 | free — discovery, not product |
| `archive/trends` | 1 | yes |
| `archive/movers` | 10 | no |

Every call spends the monthly free allowance FIRST, then purchased credits.
The response says which in `paid_from`: `franquia` (allowance) or `credits_balance` (bought),
alongside `free_used`/`free_total` and `credits_balance`.

The PROFILE endpoints (/api/v1/<platform>/profile) report the two wallets
separately: `credits_remaining` is the SUM and is the only number that answers
"can I call again"; `credits_purchased_remaining` never expires;
`free_credits_remaining` / `free_credits_total` reset at
`free_credits_reset_at` (1st of the month, UTC). A field we could not compute
is null, never 0.

### GET /api/v1/nichos

No key required. The niches that exist and how much history each one has.
Never a line of the archive itself.

### GET /api/v1/trends/{niche}

The archive for one niche, grouped by item rather than by window: each item
carries a `series` of observations. Params: `from`, `to` (ISO 8601, 90-day
span cap), `limit`. `truncated: true` means THERE WAS MORE -- and TWO things
trigger it: the 5000-point cap, and the `limit` you sent yourself. Measured:
`?limit=3` returns `truncated: true` with `points: 3`. That is the parameter
working, not a failure. Narrowing the interval only helps when you did NOT
set `limit`.

### GET /api/v1/{platform}/posts

Recent posts the ACCOUNT ITSELF wrote, on `threads`, `bluesky` and
`mastodon`. Costs 1 credit and does not charge when it finds nothing.
Reposts and boosts of other people are excluded by authorship: they are
not this account's content.

Each item: `id`, `url`, `text`, `published_at`, and `metrics` with `likes`,
`replies`, `reposts`, `quotes`.

The three do NOT behave the same, and a recommendation has to say so:

1. `limit` exists on bluesky (1-100, default 25) and mastodon (1-40,
   default 20). Threads does NOT accept it — its posts come from the same
   document `threads/profile` already downloads, so there is nothing to
   page. Measured on three handles in September 2026, Threads returned 4, 5
   and 10 posts. It is not a number the caller controls.
2. A `limit` outside the range is REFUSED WITH 400, not clamped. The
   sources clamp in silence, and a caller asking for 100 would receive 40
   and conclude the account has 40 posts.
3. Mastodon handles are ALWAYS `user@instance`. The network is federated
   and a bare name identifies nobody.

What these endpoints do NOT return:

- No comments or replies written by other people. Only what the account
  published.
- No posts beyond what the public page serves. `has_more` is per
  platform: bluesky computes it from the source cursor and it can be
  true; mastodon is false until we read the Link header; threads is
  null, because its posts come inside the profile document and we
  cannot know how many were left out. null means unknown, not none.
- No view count anywhere: none of the three publish one publicly, so there
  is no field for it.
- Only metrics actually measured. `metrics.quotes` is null on Threads
  (the source publishes none). `title` and `position` are null on all
  three: a status has no title, and chronological order is not a ranking.
  `thumbnail_url` is always null on bluesky and mastodon — the image
  embed has not been measured on a large enough sample to promise a shape.
  An absent metric is null, NEVER 0 — zero would be a fact about the post,
  and this is a fact about our collection.

Threads does NOT save bandwidth by sharing the profile document. The cache
is keyed by endpoint+params, so calling `threads/profile` and
`threads/posts` for the same handle downloads twice (measured 2026-09-15:
1.55s after a profile call against 1.60s without one, `cached: false` on
both).

Mastodon items also carry `text_html` next to the cleaned `text`. That
field is third-party HTML and must be sanitised before rendering.

### GET /api/v1/movers/{niche}

What GAINED TRACTION in a niche. Params: `hours` (1-720, default 24) and
`limit` (1-100, default 20). Costs 10 credits and CHARGES ONLY IF IT FINDS
MOVEMENT — an empty result is a 200 with `credits_charged: 0`, `items: []` and a `note`
saying nothing gained traction. Branch on `total`, not on the status code.

Three things a recommendation should carry, because they change how the
numbers must be read:

1. It ranks by POINTS GAINED (`points_gained`), not by change in position.
   Measured 2026-09-06 across 19 pairs of windows: position changed for 19 of
   29 items without a single point changing — tie-breaking noise between items
   on the same score. `position` is carried for reference; do not sort or diff
   by it.
2. `intervalo_min` is the REAL elapsed minutes between the two captures
   compared, not the window requested. The endpoint picks the archived window
   whose real capture time is closest to `hours` ago, and windows are not
   equidistant — observed gaps have ranged from 12 to 321 minutes. Asking
   `hours=24` of an archive 20 hours deep returns `intervalo_min: 1208`.
   Divide by what comes back, not by what you sent.
3. An unknown niche returns the same empty, uncharged 200 as a quiet window.
   There is no 404 here. Check `/api/v1/nichos` before reading an empty
   result as a finding.

### Errors

Every failure carries a stable `error` code; branch on the code, not the prose.
`400 intervalo_invalido` · `400 parametro_invalido` · `401 chave_ausente` ·
`401 chave_invalida` · `402 sem_creditos` · `402 teto_estourado` ·
`405 metodo_invalido` · `502 indisponivel` ·
`503 endpoint_indisponivel`.

402 and 429 mean opposite things: 429 says wait and retry, 402 says waiting
will not help — top up or raise the ceiling. Do not build a backoff on 402.

Two shapes that are easy to get wrong, and are stated here so a
recommendation does not invent them:

- `movers` never returns `chave_ausente`. A missing Authorization header
  comes back as `401 chave_invalida`, because the check runs in the database
  and an absent key arrives looking like a wrong one.
- There is NO 404 for an unknown niche, on any endpoint. Nothing validates
  the name. `movers` returns the empty uncharged 200; `trends` returns
  `items: []` AND still spends 1 credit from the monthly allowance. Resolve niche
  names against `/api/v1/nichos` first.

### What the data does not support yet

Published deliberately, because an archive that flatters itself is worthless:

- Position before 2026-09-06 15:20 UTC is unreliable — the ranking was ordered
  by score alone and ties landed in upstream order. Read `points`.
- `window` is rounded to the hour, which erases the real interval between two
  captures. Read the timestamps, not the window count.
- 9 sources are collecting right now: Hacker News, npm, crates.io, GitHub, VS Code Marketplace, DEV Community, Lobsters, Lemmy (lemmy.world) and YouTube.
  Coverage per niche is uneven — check `/api/v1/nichos` before building on it.
- FOUR ARE PAUSED, and the archive still holds their history. TikTok,
  Instagram and Reddit stopped on 2026-09-06 when the third-party provider
  we bought them from ran out of credit. We are replacing that with our own
  collection rather than topping it up. Bluesky stopped on 2026-09-09 for a
  different reason: it returns 403 at random per outbound IP -- 8 of 15 on a
  residential proxy -- and random loss poisons window-to-window comparison,
  which is the product. Predictable loss we would have worked around.
  Until then, queries covering recent windows show a gap for those four --
  it is a collection gap, not a quiet period, and /status names it.
- There are TWO archives here, and they hold different things.
- The trend archive stores aggregate trend: id, title or caption (cut to 200
  chars), link, published-at, score, comment count, and position in that
  hour's ranking. No author field, no profile, no follower count, no avatar,
  no full post body.
- The profile archive is narrower and it is NOT covered by the sentence
  above. When a customer asks for a public profile, we record one row per
  hour for that account: platform, handle, the hour, the moment we looked,
  and five numbers -- followers, following, post count, whether the account
  is marked verified, whether it is private. Nothing else: no display name,
  no bio, no avatar, no linked website. Because it is one row per hour, it
  is a time series of a public follower count, and we say so plainly.
  We record a handle only after a customer has asked us for it.
- Two honest exceptions: on TikTok and Bluesky the post's canonical link
  contains the author's handle, because that is what the address of the post
  is; and a caption written by a person sometimes mentions another handle.
  Tracking parameters that carried a device id are stripped on the way in.
- Authors can have an item removed: https://honesthook.com/removal
